Case Study

InsightView Analytics Dashboard

I created a data visualization tool that transforms complex datasets into clear, actionable insights through thoughtful information hierarchy, contextual help, and customizable views that adapt to different user expertise levels.

This dashboard balances depth and accessibility, allowing both novice users to grasp key trends and expert analysts to drill down into specific metrics without feeling overwhelmed or under-supported.

Context

Organizations increasingly rely on analytics dashboards to make data-driven decisions, but most dashboards fail at the most critical task: making complex data understandable at a glance. The InsightView platform needed to serve two distinct audiences — executives who wanted top-line trends in seconds, and analysts who needed to dive deep into granular data.

Existing analytics tools in the market either oversimplified the data (alienating expert users) or presented dense, overwhelming interfaces (intimidating casual users). The challenge was designing a single system that could flex to accommodate both mental models simultaneously — progressive disclosure without punitive complexity.

Through user research, I also discovered that most dashboard users could not explain what certain metrics meant — the data was presented, but the context needed to understand it was missing. This insight became the foundation of the redesign: every visualisation must teach as well as display.

Approach

1. Stakeholder & User Research

I interviewed 12 users across three personas — business executives, data analysts, and product managers — to map their distinct goals, data literacy levels, and decision-making workflows. The research revealed that executives spent less than 90 seconds per dashboard view, while analysts could spend hours.

2. Information Architecture & Data Modeling

I designed a three-tier information hierarchy: summary cards for at-a-glance insight, interactive charts for trend exploration, and drill-down tables for granular analysis. A card-sorting exercise with users validated that the grouping matched their mental models of how business metrics relate to one another.

3. Visual Hierarchy & Layout Design

I crafted a visual system where position, size, and color encode priority — the most critical metrics appear first and largest, with secondary data revealed progressively. Contextual tooltips and inline descriptions explain what each metric means, eliminating the "what am I looking at?" confusion.

4. Interactive Prototyping in Figma

Full interactive prototypes simulated realistic data flows — date range selection, metric filtering, and drill-down interactions. Testing the prototypes with users before development saved significant engineering time and caught interaction ambiguities early.

5. Usability Validation & Iteration

Two rounds of usability testing, each with 6 participants, validated the information hierarchy and identified areas where the progressive disclosure needed fine-tuning. Task completion for finding specific metrics improved from 65% to 92% across rounds.

Result

The InsightView redesign transformed a confusing data dump into a trusted decision-making tool. Task completion for finding specific business metrics improved from 65% to 92% across two testing rounds, with a substantial reduction in time-on-task for executive users.

The progressive disclosure model received particular praise — users described the experience as "the dashboard grows with me," rather than forcing them to adapt to the tool's complexity. Contextual help features reduced the number of support inquiries about metric definitions by an estimated 60%.

The project validated a core design principle: when dashboards teach users about their data instead of merely displaying it, they shift from being passive reporting tools to active partners in decision-making.
