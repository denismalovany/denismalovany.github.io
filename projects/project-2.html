Case Study

Volunteer Centre County

My Role

UX/UI Designer & Product Designer/Manager — only designer on the project

Team

Cross-functional: product design, project management, development, QA

Context

Volunteer Centre County (VCC) is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation serving Centre County, Pennsylvania. Its mission: provide a free, user-friendly website and app that connects local residents with nonprofits seeking volunteers and donated goods. Founded in 2018 as a Penn State Intergenerational Leadership Institute project, VCC needed a complete digital platform — both a public-facing portal for volunteer/donation discovery, and an admin interface for managing community partner listings.

As a fixed-price contract with a tight deadline, every decision mattered. The challenge was to deliver a cohesive platform that served two distinct audiences — volunteers seeking opportunities and nonprofits managing their presence — without scope creep or costly rework.

Approach

1. Stakeholder Interviews & Team Alignment

I started with in-depth stakeholder interviews to understand VCC's goals, the problems to solve, and the constraints of a non-profit operating environment. Crucially, I involved the entire project team in these sessions — developers, QA, and project manager. This meant everyone heard the client's vision firsthand, building shared understanding and reducing misinterpretation downstream.

2. Competitor Analysis & Feature Matrix

I researched direct competitors — other volunteer-matching platforms and community portals — and built a feature matrix to establish a baseline of what users would expect. This matrix helped identify the absolute minimum viable feature set, differentiate VCC from similar services, and guide scope conversations with the client.

3. Iterative Client Workshops

After each research phase, I facilitated workshops with the client to present findings, validate assumptions, and uncover additional domain knowledge. This cadence kept the client deeply embedded in the process and prevented the common trap of discovering misalignment late in the project.

4. Feature Mindmap & Sitemap

I created a comprehensive mindmap of all potential features, then distilled it into a hybrid sitemap that mapped both page hierarchy and the features living on each page. This artifact became the single source of truth for scope — and later, the blueprint for VCC's planned future improvements.

5. Scope Validation with Client & Team

I discussed the sitemap with the client to get her industry-informed perspective on both the public site and admin interface. Then I walked through the scope with the full team to determine what was realistically deliverable within the fixed budget and deadline. The team's involvement here was invaluable — they flagged out-of-scope additions before they became commitments.

6. Wireframing & Iterative Feedback

I produced low-fidelity wireframes for key pages — homepage, volunteer listings, partner profiles, donation needs, events. These were presented to the client on a regular cadence so she could provide feedback and discuss design decisions based on our collective knowledge. This iterative approach meant no surprises and continuous alignment.

7. Style Tile Exploration

I created several style tiles showcasing different visual directions — typography, colour palettes, button styles, card treatments. The first iteration was ultimately declined because it unintentionally resembled FedEx branding. The second iteration was approved and became the foundation for the full design system.

8. Figma Design System & Full UI

With the visual direction confirmed, I built a complete Figma design system — reusable components, typography scales, colour tokens, and interaction patterns. All public-facing pages were designed to spec within this system. For the admin panel, I delivered wireframes only, as it would be built on a standard framework theme.

Result

The project was delivered with minimal delays — a direct outcome of the collaborative, transparent process we established from day one. Because the entire team was involved in every design phase, everyone understood the scope, the rationale behind decisions, and when to push back on out-of-scope additions.

The client was very satisfied with the final platform. The deep involvement throughout the process meant there was zero need for post-delivery revision requests — she fully understood what had been built, why, and how it addressed her needs.

Perhaps the strongest signal of success: the client already had a clear roadmap for future improvements, based directly on the mindmap and sitemap we created together at the project's outset. The platform wasn't just delivered — it was set up to grow.

Key Takeaways

  • Involve the whole team early. Including developers in stakeholder interviews eliminated handoff friction and built collective ownership of the project goals.
  • Continuous client workshops prevent rework. Presenting work after every phase kept the client in the loop and eliminated end-of-project surprises.
  • Scope discipline is a team sport. When the whole team understands the scope, everyone becomes a guardrail against creep.
  • Iterate on visual direction early. The first style tile was rejected — but catching it early meant minimal wasted effort and a stronger final direction.
  • Design for the next version too. The mindmap and sitemap doubled as a future roadmap, giving the client a clear path forward after launch.
