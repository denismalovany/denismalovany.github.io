CoffeeNanny Lviv Franchise Website

CoffeeNanny is a service that helps families find trusted nannies for their children — by the hour or for longer periods, both in-home and onsite. When I came on board through a local Lviv agency, the franchise had a critical trust problem: without a proper website, serious clients had no way to verify the service or feel confident entrusting their children to a new provider.

Social media worked for discovery, but families needed transparency, clear pricing, and a professional channel to make an informed decision. I led the entire digital rebuild — from UX audit and client interviews to Figma design and a rapid Wix MVP.

Context

The original franchise site — built for the Kyiv office — went down during the full-scale invasion. The Lviv franchise needed a new digital presence urgently, but more importantly, they needed one that actually worked for their local market. Research revealed the core issue wasn't just missing functionality — it was trust.

Most local leads came through Instagram, but Instagram alone couldn't close serious clients. There was also a new audience: many displaced families who had fled to Lviv from other regions were searching for nannies directly on Google — they had no local Instagram network to discover CoffeeNanny through. Without a website, this entire segment was completely unreachable.

Families needed to see pricing, understand how the service worked, and feel confident before trusting someone with their child. A proper website wasn't just nice to have — it was the only channel that could serve both local and displaced families effectively.

Approach

1. UX Audit & Competitive Research

I started with a UX audit of the previous franchise-wide site. Key finding: it felt generic — no local presence, no Lviv-specific content, no clear trust signals for a childcare service. I also researched direct and indirect competitors to identify what they did well and where CoffeeNanny could differentiate. This analysis shaped our positioning and content strategy from day one.

2. Client & Stakeholder Interviews

I interviewed existing clients to understand what drove their trust, how they discovered the service, and what nearly stopped them from booking. Conversations with the franchise owner clarified business priorities and the roadmap.

3. Design in Figma + AI-Assisted Style Exploration

Based on research insights, I designed the full site in Figma — information architecture, wireframes, and high-fidelity UI. To speed things up, I used one of the first AI design tools available in 2022 to generate style variants and explore different visual directions rapidly. This let us converge on the right look much faster than iterating manually.

4. MVP on Wix

To ship fast, I built the MVP directly on Wix — leveraging its CMS, SEO tools, and hosting to get the site live in days rather than weeks. This allowed the franchise to immediately start generating inquiries.

5. Data-Informed Iteration

After launch, I analysed user behaviour and conversion data to refine the experience — A/B testing messaging, adjusting the booking flow, and improving content based on what actually drove inquiries.

Result

The business went from near-collapse to stable growth. The new site launched in days and became a working client-generation engine. Four years later, the site is still live and the franchise has expanded to 9 cities across Ukraine.

Traffic Growth (May 2022 → 2026)

The site launched with almost no traffic and a 90% bounce rate. Within months it was attracting thousands of visitors per month — a 20× increase in traffic driven entirely by organic search and social media, with no paid advertising.

Engagement Quality

The bounce rate dropped from 90% at launch to ~55% — a clear signal that the UX and content structure worked: visitors were finding what they needed instead of leaving immediately. Average session duration grew to 2–4 minutes, indicating serious engagement from potential clients.

The project proved that a lean, research-informed approach can keep a business alive under extraordinary pressure — and that a well-designed MVP can become a permanent, revenue-generating asset.
