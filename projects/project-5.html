Case Study

ResearchLens Data Visualization Tool

I developed a specialized visualization platform for scientific researchers that transforms complex experimental data into clear visual narratives, helping scientists identify patterns, validate hypotheses, and communicate findings more effectively.

By combining rigorous data accuracy with thoughtful visual storytelling, this tool bridges the gap between complex analysis and accessible interpretation, supporting both detailed exploration and clear presentation of research findings.

Context

Scientific researchers generate enormous volumes of experimental data, but the tools available to visualize and communicate that data are often arcane — built by scientists for scientists, with steep learning curves and little consideration for readability or aesthetics. The gap between "accurate data" and "understandable data" is where ResearchLens operates.

Researchers needed to do two things equally well: explore their data deeply during analysis (requiring precision and flexibility), and present their findings clearly to peers and funders (requiring clarity and narrative). Most existing tools forced them to choose one mode or the other, creating a split workflow that introduced friction and errors.

The core design challenge was building a tool that treated data visualization as storytelling — not just as a technical output. Every chart needed to answer a question, and every interaction needed to bring the researcher closer to an insight.

Approach

1. Domain Immersion & Researcher Interviews

I spent two weeks embedded with a molecular biology research team, observing their workflow from raw data to publication-ready figures. I interviewed 15 researchers across 4 disciplines to understand their pain points, toolchains, and mental models around data analysis and visual communication.

2. Data Schema Design & Visualization Strategy

Working with the research teams, I mapped common data types and experiment structures to appropriate visualization strategies. The key insight: researchers needed "guided modes" that suggested the right chart type based on their data structure, reducing the paradox of choice that paralyzed non-data-specialist scientists.

3. Dashboard Architecture & Wireframing

I designed a dual-mode architecture: an "exploration" mode with dense, interactive charts supporting deep analysis, and a "presentation" mode with simplified, narrative-focused views for sharing with peers. A single toggle switched between modes, preserving the underlying data context.

4. High-Fidelity Design in Figma

I crafted a visual language that balanced scientific credibility with modern clarity — using consistent color scales, clear typographic hierarchy, and subtle visual cues to guide attention to the most statistically significant findings first. Every chart included plain-language annotations explaining what the data showed.

5. Expert Review & Iterative Refinement

The design went through three rounds of expert review with senior researchers, iterating on the chart annotation system, the exploration/presentation toggle, and the guided visualization recommendations. Each round produced measurable improvements in first-time task completion.

Result

ResearchLens successfully bridged the gap between raw data exploration and polished scientific communication. Researchers reported spending 35% less time preparing publication-ready figures, and the guided visualization recommendations reduced chart selection errors by over 50% among non-specialist users.

The exploration/presentation toggle was the most praised feature — researchers described it as "finally having one tool that does both jobs." The plain-language annotations on charts helped junior researchers understand statistical concepts they had previously found opaque, creating an unexpected educational benefit.

The project validated a broader design insight: domain-specific tools don't need to sacrifice usability for power. When designers deeply immerse themselves in the user's world, they can build interfaces that feel inevitable — as if the tool was always meant to work this way.

My Role
